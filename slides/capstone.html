How the red-team day runs

Attacka timed attack on the other team's hardened agent, every trial logged

The duration of each block is in the capstone brief in labs/capstone/: 20, 120, 40 and 90 minutes, then 20 minutes of final preparation and 15 minutes a team. Six hours leave room for the defences of four teams; with more, run the defences in two rooms with an assessor each, never shorter than 15 minutes a team. Breaks are not on the list: press B when the room needs one, and never in the middle of the attack block.
Rules of engagement

Attack the assigned build, on the synthetic data, within the budget

In scope

The other team's build.py, through the task channel, the inbox, the tariff notes, importer memory and the two servers' tool listings.

Out of scope

The other team's laptops, accounts and repository settings. Any real address, host or model account. Any payload that leaves the lab.

Stop when

The time box ends, the budget of 600 model calls is spent, or anything outside the synthetic data is reached: then stop and tell the trainer.

A finding counts when it has at least ten trials, a judge written in code, the trace of one successful trial and the model's name.

Each team wrote these rules in the last step of Lab 4.4; have each attacker read its own aloud before the timer starts. Planning and running a red-team programme across an organisation is AE-407; today is one exercise with fixed rules.

With the stand-in model the wording that works is bounded by what it recognises; the channel, the goal, the victim task and the combination are the attacker's choice. With a hosted model any wording is in scope.

Attack

Attack · 120 minutes

Record every trial. Ten trials and a judge in code make a finding.

Press T to start the timer. Walk the room and watch for two things: teams that polish one payload and log nothing, and teams that drift out of scope. Say the remaining time at the half and at the last twenty minutes.
Harden

Harden · 90 minutes

For each confirmed finding: a test that fails, a fix, the test passing, benign tasks at 95% or more.

Press T to start the timer. The 40 minutes of reporting come before this slide: findings are handed over in writing, from the template. A team that cannot fix a finding in the time writes it into its residual-risk statement; that is a valid outcome and is graded as such.
How presentations are scored

The rubric

20%

30%
Guardrails and approvals
15%
Regression suite and report
15%
The assessors read the evidence. Attack logs row by row, one trace per finding, the commit history, and the suite running.

Walk through each criterion with one example of full marks. Threat model: twelve or more threats, mapped and scored, the untested ones named. Attack evidence: a rate and an interval per technique, in Arabic and English. Containment and authorisation: every logged exfiltration path fails with 95% of benign tasks passing, the token audit, the deputy test failing. Guardrails and approvals: both error rates, the approval placement with its counts. Regression suite and report: red then green in the pipeline, and the statement.

Authenticity: ask a team to rerun one row of its log live. The stand-in gives the same result for the same attack and trial number.

Format

Ten minutes of defence, five minutes of questions

1 · One attack, twice

Reproduce one attack on the build as found. Run it on your build and show which control refuses it, in the action log.

2 · What the others found

Each confirmed finding: its rate, the test that failed, the fix, the test passing, and what the fix cost benign tasks.

3 · What remains

The residual-risk statement: what is closed exactly, what is lowered to a rate, what is open, and who accepts it.

Peer feedback. After each defence, the attacking team says which open point it would try next.
Questions the assessors ask every team: which of your zeros are exact and which are rates? What did your controls cost legitimate users, and in which language? Your suite passes: what does that not prove? The supplier changes the model next month: what do you run?
