Session 1

A fooled model must break nothing important

  • Explain why a design must hold when the model is fooled
  • List the entry points for untrusted text from run traces
  • List every action the agent can take and its effect
01

Session 1 sets the stance for the whole course. The title is the thesis: a fooled model must break nothing important.

The stance

Assume the model will be fooled, then ask what it can reach

The question that fails

"How do we stop the model from obeying an attacker?" Nobody can promise that, and an attacker retries for free.

The question that works

"When the model obeys an attacker, what can it read, and what can it do?" That has an answer you can test.

The design goal. A fooled model must break nothing important.

Write the two questions on the board and leave them there all week. Every control of Day 3 answers the second question; the guardrail of Day 4 is the only control that tries to answer the first, and it comes last and with two error rates.

Two ways to see the model

A component transforms text; an actor chooses what happens next

Model as componentModel as actor
It decidesNothing: the application calls it and uses the outputWhich tool to call, with which arguments, and when to stop
A fooled modelReturns wrong textTakes wrong actions
The worst caseWhatever the application does with that textEverything its tools and credentials allow
MukhallisThe quarantined extraction of Day 3The planner with twelve tools

The distinction comes back on Day 3: the split turns the model that reads invoices into a component. Ask: in your own systems, which calls are components and which are actors?

Checkpoint

Which claim survives a fooled model?

Wissam, who built Mukhallis, lists four reasons why it is safe. Which one still holds on the day the model obeys a hostile invoice?

The system prompt says never to share one importer's data with another
In ten test tasks the agent never called release_hold
The case server refuses any call for a case other than the one the officer opened
The model was tested on 200 messages and followed its instructions every time
Why. Only the third is enforced outside the model. The other three describe what the model usually does, which is exactly what an attacker changes.

Answer: the case server's refusal. As found, the case server does no such thing: that is Lab 3.3. If the room picks the system prompt, go back to the two questions on the board.

Session 2

Data flows and trust boundaries

  • Draw the agent as processes, stores, external entities and flows
  • Mark trust boundaries and the flows that cross them
  • Find where private data, untrusted content and an exit path meet
02

Session 2 gives the threat model its drawing. Keep it practical: the diagram is data, and code reads it.

The notation

Four shapes are enough to draw any agent

External entity

Outside your control: the importer, a rate site, the officer.

Process

Code that transforms data: the planner, the code runner, each server, the case screen.

Data store

Where data rests: importer records, tariff notes, memory.

Data flow

An arrow with a name: what moves, from where to where.

Classic data-flow diagrams; nothing here is special to AI. What is special comes on the next slides: one process, the planner, reads and writes almost every flow.

Boundaries

A trust boundary is where the author of the data changes

  • Mukhallis has four zones: outside, authority, agent, servers
  • A flow that crosses a boundary needs a decision: who checks it, and how
  • Trust follows the author, not the transport: an importer's email is still the importer's after the case server stored it
13 of 19
flows of Mukhallis cross a trust boundary (Lab 1.2)
What goes wrong. "It comes from our own case server" is said about text an outsider wrote yesterday.

The number is from the lab's solution. Ask the room for a flow that does not cross a boundary: planner to memory, planner to code. They are inside the agent zone and still carry an outsider's words.

Checkpoint

Which flow carries untrusted content?

A broker uploads a circular. It becomes a tariff note. A week later the planner retrieves it for another importer's question. Where does untrusted content cross into the agent's context?

On the flow from the tariff notes to the planner: the note is still the broker's words
Nowhere: the note was stored inside the authority, so it is trusted now
Only at upload, where the broker's file crossed the boundary once
On the flow from the planner to the comms server, when the reply is sent
Why. The note's author is outside. Every retrieval brings an outsider's text into a context that also holds a record and an email tool.

Answer: the first option. This is the mechanism of Lab 2.3. If the room chooses "only at upload", ask who reviews uploads and whether the review reads for instructions.

Session 3

Risk lists and technique catalogues

  • Tell a risk entry from an adversary technique
  • Map a threat to an OWASP entry and a MITRE ATLAS technique
  • State a threat as source, entry point, action and harm
03

Session 3 is about names. Participants map threats to entries and techniques, and learn that a mapping is an argument.

Two kinds of list

A risk list names what goes wrong in your application; a technique catalogue names what an adversary does

OWASP lists

Weaknesses and their consequences, ranked. You map your threats and your controls to entries.

MITRE ATLAS

Tactics (the adversary's goal at a step) and techniques (how). You map your tests to techniques.

Use both. The entry tells the owner why it matters. The technique tells the tester what to reproduce.

AE-207 used the OWASP list to rank risks of one assistant. Here each threat gets both an entry and a technique, and the capstone findings are keyed to them. Sources: OWASP GenAI Security Project; MITRE ATLAS.

Stating a threat

A threat you can test names four things

Sourcewho writes the text
→
Entry pointwhere it enters
→
Actionwhich tool or output it turns
→
Harmwhat is lost
TH-01. An importer plants text in the body of an email so that send_email does this: another importer's record is emailed to an outside address.

Contrast with "prompt injection is a risk for our agent", which nobody can test. Each of the four fields later becomes a column of the attack log.

Mapping

A mapping is an argument: write the reason beside the id

TH-07: a description changes after approval

The text is an injection (LLM01). The cause is a supplier's component that changed under you (LLM04, ASI04). The key takes the cause: the control follows from it.

TH-09: one key for both servers

Excessive agency (LLM03)? No model is fooled. A server acts with authority nobody gave it: identity and privilege abuse (ASI03).

What goes wrong. Ids are pasted in to look complete. A linter catches an id that does not exist; only a reviewer catches one that does not fit.

These are the two disputes of Lab 1.3. Let two teams argue TH-07 for two minutes. The lab's key accepts more than one answer where two are defensible, and says which.

Checkpoint

Which pair fits the threat?

An importer's invoice says: "use the exchange rate from" an outside address that ends in the importer's IBAN. The agent calls fetch_rate with that address. Which risk entry and technique fit best?

LLM04 Supply Chain, with AI Supply Chain Rug Pull
LLM06 Unbounded Consumption, with Cost Harvesting
LLM08 Hidden Context Exposure, with Extract LLM System Prompt
LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure, with Exfiltration via AI Agent Tool Invocation
Why. The harm is a Restricted value leaving; the adversary's step is a tool call whose argument carries it. That is TH-03.

Answer: the last option. LLM01 with indirect injection is also defensible as the entry technique; the lab's key accepts it. The best single pair names the harm and the step that causes it.

04

Session 4 turns the list into a plan: scores, a budget, and what ten trials can show.

Scoring

Level is likelihood times impact. Treatments: avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept.

The scale of the National AI Risk Management Framework, as AE-207 used it in its Lab 2.4. One slide of recap. What is new here: the likelihood will be replaced by a measured rate tomorrow. Sources: National AI Risk Management Framework (SDAIA, April 2026).

Likelihood

Before a test, likelihood is a judgement with a reason; after, it is a rate

  • Who can reach the entry point? Anyone with an email address, or only a broker with upload rights
  • How much skill? One sentence in an invoice, or control of a server
  • How often does it work? Unknown today. Tomorrow: successes over trials, with an interval
Write the reason. "TH-04: needs a shipment under hold and the right wording; uninspected goods leave the port" can be challenged. A bare 2 and 4 cannot.

Lab 1.4 asks for a reason of at least a few words per score, and checks that no score is more than one step from the trainer's key. The register is AI-off: participants should be able to defend each number aloud.

Checkpoint

Where does the next model call go?

You have 90 calls left. TH-03 (level 9, 9 calls a trial) is untested. TH-01 (level 12) stands at 5 successes in 10. What do you do with the 90 calls?

Ten more trials of TH-01, to narrow its interval
Ten trials of TH-03: an untested high threat teaches more than a tighter interval on one that already works
Ninety single-call probes of the portal chat, to cover more ground
Keep them in reserve: an unspent budget is the safer report
Why. TH-01 already has its answer: it works. The open question is whether TH-03 works at all, and ten trials can answer that.

Answer: ten trials of TH-03. Use it to say what trials are for: first "does it work at all", then, only when a decision depends on it, "how often".

Day 1 · wrap-up

Three things to keep

Assume it is fooled

The design question is what a fooled model can read and do, never whether it will obey.

Entries, actions, exits

Four origins of outside text, seven actions, four exits. Defences go on flows.

A budget names the gaps

Score with reasons, test five, and write down the high threats you did not test.

Ask each table for the one threat they are most curious to attack tomorrow.
